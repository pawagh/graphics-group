We have been working on the challenging problem of 3D reconstruction of room-sized dynamic scenes (i.e., containing moving humans and objects) with a quality and fidelity significantly higher than currently possible. Visualization of these 3D reconstructions on virtual and augmented reality displays can provide an immersive experience for activities currently requiring live physical presence. Currently we are working on 3D capture of prostate biopsies and trauma surgical procedures that can provide an immersive learning experience of medical surgeries.

These improvements will enable immersive learning of rare and important situations through post-event, annotated, guided virtual reality experiences. Examples of such situations include emergency medical procedures. We expect to have a significant impact on society by enabling such immersive experiences for activities currently requiring live physical presence.
